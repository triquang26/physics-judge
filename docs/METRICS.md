# Metrics

Every metric this package registers, generated by reading each
`src/kinescore/metrics/*.py` module's `MetricSpec`. See
`src/kinescore/core/metric.py` for the frozen `MetricSpec`/`MetricValue`
contract this table follows: a metric that cannot be computed returns `NaN`
with a `reason` — **never `0.0`**, which would read as "perfect". `dt_exponent`
is the power of `1/dt` the metric's value scales by (speed = 1, accel = 2,
jerk = 3, a purely geometric quantity = 0); every declared exponent is
verified numerically by `tests/test_metric_registry_conformance.py`, so a
wrong declaration here would fail CI.

There are **28** registered metrics across 8 modules:
`rigidity` (4), `temporal` (5), `angular` (3), `energy` (3),
`joint_limits` (3), `joint_dynamics` (4), `smoothness` (2),
`feasibility` (4). `src/kinescore/metrics/suites.py::INVARIANT_V1` is the
shared suite all 28 belong to; 10 of them additionally form the PIS
(`invariant_keys`) — see [ADDING_A_METRIC.md](ADDING_A_METRIC.md) for what
changing that set costs.

Legend: **PIS** = in `INVARIANT_V1.invariant_keys` (the Physical Invariance
Score's fixed term set). **perframe** = also emits a per-frame array for
distributional (W1) comparison (`MetricSuite.quantity_keys`).

## rigidity — `src/kinescore/metrics/rigidity.py`

Static geometry: does each frame draw a rigid robot of the right link
lengths. Requires `P`.

### `rigidity_residual_mm`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean(|realised_bone_length - rest_length|) * 1000` over
  frames, keypoints, and bones in `robot.rigid_bone_pairs`.
- **Units.** mm. **`dt` exponent.** `0` (purely geometric, no derivative).
  **Direction.** lower_better.
- **Detects.** A drawn robot whose link lengths don't match its own rest
  geometry — the arm visibly stretching, shrinking, or disjointing.
- **Does NOT detect.** Anything about *speed* — a static per-frame check, so
  a link that is wrong by a constant amount in every frame (not a wobble)
  contributes identically whether the clip is fast or slow. Under
  `bone_set="all"` it is **contaminated by gripper actuation** — see
  `rigidity_residual_all_mm` below and defect D9 in
  [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md#d9--gripper-contaminates-rigidity-found-during-this-migration):
  a perfectly rigid, motionless Franka arm holding its gripper open scores
  **15.37 mm** under the legacy bone set (0.00 mm closed; 7.2 mm for a
  ramped 0→1 open) purely from the gripper-finger bones, none of which is a
  rigidity defect.

### `rigidity_residual_all_mm`

- Identical formula to `rigidity_residual_mm`, but over `robot.bone_pairs`
  (the *full*, un-filtered consecutive-keypoint set — degenerate/actuated
  bones included). Exists **only** to reproduce legacy numbers for
  provenance comparison — never register this in a new suite meant to score
  grasping clips. Not in PIS.
- **Does NOT detect** genuine arm rigidity for any robot whose consecutive
  keypoint chain includes a non-arm actuator (the Franka gripper is the
  known case; see `robots/franka/constants.py::ACTUATED_LINKS`) — its number
  is dominated by that actuator's own motion, quoted above (15.37 mm).

### `rigidity_wobble_mm`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean(bone_length.std(dim=time, unbiased=False)) * 1000` over
  `robot.rigid_bone_pairs`. Population std (`ddof=0`), not the sample std the
  source used for its analogous quantity — deliberately, because there is no
  sampling story under which "T observed frames are a sample from a larger
  population" makes sense here, so a Bessel correction only buys a spurious
  pole at small T.
- **Units.** mm. **`dt` exponent.** `0`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 2 (an unbiased std at T=1 is NaN and would poison any
  mean it feeds; the population statistic sidesteps the Bessel pole but T≥2
  is still required for the statistic to mean anything).
- **Detects.** Flicker/wobble of the drawn arm's link lengths over time,
  independent of a constant reader localisation bias (a systematic offset in
  every keypoint cancels in a length *difference*, so this metric isolates
  temporal instability from absolute reader accuracy).
- **Does NOT detect.** A constant, time-invariant length error (that's what
  `rigidity_residual_mm` is for); under `bone_set="all"`, same gripper
  contamination as above.

### `rigidity_wobble_all_mm`

- Same relationship to `rigidity_wobble_mm` as `rigidity_residual_all_mm` has
  to `rigidity_residual_mm` — legacy reproduction only, `bone_set="all"`.

## temporal — `src/kinescore/metrics/temporal.py`

Linear dynamics on keypoint trajectories (speed/accel/jerk via
`metrics/ops.py::fd`, applied 1/2/3 times). Requires `P`.

### `mean_speed_mps`

- **Formula.** `mean(||dP/dt||)` over frames and keypoints.
- **Units.** m/s. **`dt` exponent.** `1`. **Direction.** lower_better.
- **Detects.** Overall motion magnitude relative to a reference speed
  distribution (via W1, not this raw number alone — see
  [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md), PIS caveat below).
- **Does NOT detect.** Whether the speed is *appropriate* for the task — a
  fast wipe and a slow insertion are both physically plausible at very
  different speeds, which is exactly why this is a task-*dependent*
  descriptive magnitude, not a PIS term (see `metrics/suites.py`'s
  reasoning for what is/isn't in `invariant_keys`).

### `mean_accel_mps2`

- **Formula.** `mean(||d²P/dt²||)`.
- **Units.** m/s². **`dt` exponent.** `2`. **Direction.** lower_better.
- **Detects.** Average acceleration magnitude — a coarse smoothness proxy.
- **Does NOT detect.** A single sharp spike buried in an otherwise smooth
  clip (a mean can hide one outlier frame; see `max_accel_mps2` and
  `mean_jerk_mps3` for that).

### `max_accel_mps2`

- **Formula.** `max(||d²P/dt²||)` over frames and keypoints.
- **Units.** m/s². **`dt` exponent.** `2`. **Direction.** lower_better.
- **Detects.** The single worst instantaneous acceleration in the clip —
  catches an isolated pop/teleport that a mean would average away.
- **Does NOT detect.** Whether that worst moment is *typical* — one bad frame
  in an otherwise perfect 200-frame clip scores identically to one bad frame
  in a 10-frame clip.

### `mean_jerk_mps3`  — **PIS**, **perframe**

- **Formula.** `mean(||d³P/dt³||)`.
- **Units.** m/s³. **`dt` exponent.** `3`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 4.
- **Detects.** The single most sensitive un-physicality signal in the
  package for raw magnitude: a generator that pops the arm between frames has
  *bounded* velocity (position doesn't run away) but *explosive* jerk. Emits
  a per-frame trace for distributional (W1) comparison.
- **Does NOT detect.** A smoothly-executed but kinematically impossible
  motion (e.g. a joint sweeping through a physical obstruction smoothly) —
  jerk only measures the third derivative's magnitude, not whether the path
  itself is realisable; is not scale-invariant across clips of different
  speed/duration/amplitude (see `sparc`/`log_dimensionless_jerk` below for
  the dimensionless alternative).

### `accel_violation_frac`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean((||d²P/dt²|| > accel_bound).float())` over
  frames×keypoints, `accel_bound=5.0` m/s² by default (a Franka-EE-under-
  normal-teleop-conservative constant, not derived from `dt`).
- **Units.** fraction. **`dt` exponent.** `None` — **not a power law**: it
  thresholds a `dt`-dependent quantity (acceleration) against a
  **fixed physical constant** that does not move with `dt`. **Direction.**
  lower_better.
- **Detects.** Excessive acceleration relative to a hard, absolute physical
  ceiling — a threshold crossing, not a scaled comparison.
- **Does NOT detect** *anything reliably at the wrong frame rate*: this is
  **the metric most sensitive to a wrong `dt` of any in the package**. A
  measured 2x `dt` error (the literal D1 scenario) inflates acceleration 4x
  and drives this fraction from `0.000` to `0.3875` on a representative
  trajectory (`tests/test_dt_invariance.py::test_wrong_dt_is_detected`) — a
  near-discontinuous jump, not a gentle nudge, which is precisely why it is
  excluded from the `dt_exponent` conformance check rather than assigned a
  misleading power.

## angular — `src/kinescore/metrics/angular.py`

Angular dynamics on per-link rotations. Requires `R` (`Capability.ROTATIONS`).
`angular_velocity` is a **small-angle estimate**
(`omega ≈ vee(skew(R_{t+1} R_t^T)) / dt`), valid only while `|omega|*dt << 1`
per frame — true at any normal control rate for a physically actuated link,
not enforced anywhere in this module (inherited unchanged from the source).

### `mean_angvel_radps`

- **Formula.** `mean(||omega||)` over frames and links.
- **Units.** rad/s. **`dt` exponent.** `1`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 2.
- **Detects.** Overall angular speed magnitude, descriptive (like
  `mean_speed_mps`).
- **Does NOT detect.** Correctness at a badly subsampled (very large `dt`) or
  very fast-spinning trajectory — the small-angle approximation silently
  *degrades* (underestimates the true rate) outside its validity bound rather
  than erroring.

### `max_angvel_radps`

- **Formula.** `max(||omega||)`.
- **Units.** rad/s. **`dt` exponent.** `1`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 2.
- **Detects.** Worst single-frame angular speed.
- **Does NOT detect.** Whether that worst moment is typical (same caveat as
  `max_accel_mps2`); same small-angle validity bound as above.

### `mean_angacc_radps2`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean(||d(omega)/dt||)`.
- **Units.** rad/s². **`dt` exponent.** `2`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 3. The one angular term the source's own
  `INVARIANT_KEYS` actually used — mean/max angular *speed* are
  task-dependent magnitudes, not invariant residuals.
- **Detects.** Rotational jerkiness of link orientation — the rotational
  analogue of `mean_accel_mps2`.
- **Does NOT detect.** `omega` is itself a small-angle estimate, so this
  inherits the same validity-bound caveat one derivative further removed.

## energy — `src/kinescore/metrics/energy.py`

Energy/momentum continuity, unit point masses on keypoints — **proxies, not
physical quantities of the real robot** (whose links have real, non-uniform
mass); used as smoothness/continuity signals, exactly as the source used
them. Requires `P`.

### `kinetic_energy_tstd`

- **Formula.** `KE_t = 0.5 * sum_k ||v_k||^2` per frame;
  `mean(KE.std(dim=time))` (default/unbiased std).
- **Units.** (m/s)². **`dt` exponent.** `2`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 3 (keeps the underlying T-1-length KE sequence at ≥2
  samples so the unbiased std isn't NaN by construction).
- **Detects.** A physical motion changes kinetic energy smoothly; a
  flickering/popping generator spikes this.
- **Does NOT detect.** Real kinetic energy of the robot — unit point masses,
  not the robot's actual link inertias.

### `total_energy_tstd`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `total = KE + g*sum_k(z_k)` (unit-mass PE, `g=9.81`);
  `mean(total.std(dim=time))`.
- **Units.** (m/s)². **`dt` exponent.** **`None`** — **not dimensionally
  homogeneous**: it sums a `dt^-2`-scaling kinetic term with a `dt^0`
  (derivative-free) potential term, so there is no single exponent `n` for
  which `total(k*dt) == total(dt)/k**n` — the two terms would need
  *different* `n`. **Measured**: a 2x `dt` error shifts this metric by
  **1.397x**, not the clean 4x a pure `dt_exponent=2` metric would show, and
  not the 1x a pure `dt_exponent=0` metric would show either. **Direction.**
  lower_better. **`min_frames`.** 3.
- **Detects.** Same continuity idea as `kinetic_energy_tstd`, plus a
  height-based potential term.
- **Does NOT detect.** A clean power-law relationship with frame rate — this
  is the headline non-homogeneous metric in the package; treat any
  cross-`dt` comparison of this specific key as unreliable by an amount that
  does not follow a simple rule.

### `momentum_dp_mean`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `p = sum_k v_k` (unit-mass linear momentum);
  `mean(||d(p)/dt||)`.
- **Units.** m/s². **`dt` exponent.** `2` (velocity's one power of `1/dt`,
  plus one more from the finite difference building `dp`). **Direction.**
  lower_better. **`min_frames`.** 3.
- **Detects.** A net-force-magnitude proxy: large jumps in system momentum
  with no visible contact are unphysical.
- **Does NOT detect.** Real net force — unit point masses, not the robot's
  actual mass distribution.

## joint_limits — `src/kinescore/metrics/joint_limits.py`

Joint-limit feasibility. Retargeted at `q_raw`, not `q` — see
[PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md#d7--limit_violation-structurally-always-0) (D7).

### `limit_violation_frac`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `excess = relu(q_raw-hi) + relu(lo-q_raw)`;
  `mean((excess.sum(-1) > 0).float())` over frames.
- **Units.** fraction. **`dt` exponent.** `0` (per-frame comparison against
  static limits, no derivative). **Direction.** lower_better.
  **Requires.** `q_raw`. **`unobservable_when`.**
  `limit_semantics=squashed`.
- **Detects.** A `"raw_rad"` reader's joint reading exceeding the URDF's
  declared `[q_lo, q_hi]` in at least one joint, for a frame.
- **Does NOT detect anything at all under a squashed-head reader** — it is
  **structurally `0`** by construction of the head, not by anything about the
  video (the whole of defect D7). kinescore reports this as `null`
  (`NaN` + `"unobservable:limit_semantics=squashed"`), **never `0`**, for
  every squashed-head reader — which is every real production checkpoint
  today (`judge_v3l`, `judge_v3l_mv`, `judge_reward`).

### `limit_excess_rad`

- **Formula.** `mean(excess.sum(-1))` over frames (same `excess` as above).
- **Units.** rad. **`dt` exponent.** `0`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **Requires.** `q_raw`. **`unobservable_when`.** `limit_semantics=squashed`.
- **Detects.** *How much* a `"raw_rad"` reader's joints overshoot the limits
  by, on average — the clamp magnitude on the unsquashed reading **is** the
  signal.
- **Does NOT detect** anything under a squashed head — same caveat as
  `limit_violation_frac`, `null` not `0`.

### `limit_headroom_rad`

- **Formula.** Per frame, `min_j(min(hi_j - q_j, q_j - lo_j))` (tightest
  per-joint margin, minimum over joints); `mean` of that worst-case-per-frame
  margin over the clip. Uses the always-safe `q`, never `q_raw`.
- **Units.** rad. **`dt` exponent.** `0`. **Direction.** **higher_better** —
  the one metric in this package where more is better.
- **Detects.** How close a trajectory gets to a mechanical extreme, observable
  under **any** `limit_semantics` — the closest a squashed head can express
  "did this look like it was straining against its limits" even though it can
  never literally cross them.
- **Does NOT detect** an actual violation (it structurally cannot, by
  definition — a squashed `q` is always inside `[lo,hi]`); complements, does
  not replace, `limit_violation_frac`/`limit_excess_rad` for a `"raw_rad"`
  reader.

## joint_dynamics — `src/kinescore/metrics/joint_dynamics.py`

Joint-space kinematics/feasibility from `q`.

### `mean_qdot_radps`

- **Formula.** `mean(|dq/dt|)` over joints and frames.
- **Units.** rad/s. **`dt` exponent.** `1`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **Requires.** `q`. **`min_frames`.** 2.
- **Detects.** Joint-space analogue of `mean_speed_mps`.
- **Does NOT detect.** Whether the rate is appropriate for the task (same
  descriptive-not-invariant caveat as `mean_speed_mps`).

### `mean_qddot_radps2`

- **Formula.** `mean(|d²q/dt²|)`.
- **Units.** rad/s². **`dt` exponent.** `2`. **Direction.** lower_better.
  **Requires.** `q`. **`min_frames`.** 3.
- **Detects.** Joint-space analogue of `mean_accel_mps2`.
- **Does NOT detect.** A single spike hidden inside the mean (no `max_qddot`
  variant is registered).

### `vel_violation_frac`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean((|dq/dt| > robot.vel_limits).float())` over
  frames×joints.
- **Units.** fraction. **`dt` exponent.** **`None`** — thresholds `dq/dt`
  (`dt`-dependent) against `robot.vel_limits` (a fixed, URDF-declared,
  `dt`-independent constant); same non-power-law reasoning as
  `accel_violation_frac`. **Direction.** lower_better. **Requires.** `q`,
  plus `robot.vel_limits` **checked by hand in `_compute`**, not via
  `spec.requires` (`vel_limits` is not one of the standard `Requires` input
  names in `core/metric.py`).
- **Detects.** A joint moving faster than its rated velocity — a real
  feasibility violation for that robot.
- **Does NOT detect.** Anything reliably at the wrong frame rate (same
  threshold-crossing sensitivity as `accel_violation_frac`); `NaN` with
  reason `missing_input:vel_limits`, **never `0.0`**, for a robot whose URDF
  doesn't declare joint velocity limits (e.g. the `Synthetic2R` test robot).

### `effort_proxy`  — **PIS**

- **Formula.** `mean(sum_j |d²q_j/dt²| / effort_limits_j)` over frames.
- **Units.** rad/(N·m·s²). **`dt` exponent.** `2` (`q_ddot`'s usual `dt^-2`;
  `effort_j` is a `dt`-independent URDF constant, so — unlike the two
  violation fractions in this module, which threshold — *dividing* by a
  constant preserves the power law). **Direction.** lower_better.
  **Requires.** `q`, plus `robot.effort_limits` checked by hand.
- **Detects.** A relative actuator-load indicator: low-rated-effort joints
  (typically wrist joints) cost more per unit acceleration than
  high-rated-effort ones.
- **Does NOT detect real torque** — **there is no inertia model here.** This
  is `|acceleration| / rated_effort`, not
  `torque = inertia @ acceleration + ...`. Use it **comparatively only**
  (across clips scored with the same robot and the same `dt`), never as an
  absolute torque estimate — the source's own docstring makes the same
  caveat and this port preserves it verbatim. `NaN` with reason
  `missing_input:effort_limits`, never `0.0`, for a robot without
  URDF-declared effort limits (e.g. `Fourier GR-1` — `GR1FK` has no effort
  data at all, so `GR1Spec.effort_limits is None` always).

## smoothness — `src/kinescore/metrics/smoothness.py`

Dimensionless, scale-invariant movement-smoothness metrics, aggregated
(nan-aware mean) over `robot.ee_sites()`. Pure numpy, fp64, no FK dependency
— citable literature metrics, not ad hoc.

### `sparc`

- **Formula.** Spectral Arc Length (Balasubramanian, Melendez-Calderon,
  Roby-Brami & Burdet, *"On the analysis of movement smoothness"*, J.
  NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation 12:112, 2015) of each end-effector's
  speed profile.
- **Units.** dimensionless (negative; closer to 0 = smoother). **`dt`
  exponent.** **`None`** — builds its frequency axis from `fs=1/dt` and keeps
  only bins below a **fixed absolute** cutoff `fc=10 Hz`; changing `dt`
  changes *which portion of the same normalised spectrum* the arc-length sum
  is taken over, not a clean multiplicative scaling — the same
  absolute-constant failure mode as the violation fractions above, just via a
  frequency cutoff rather than a magnitude threshold. **Direction.**
  higher_better. **`min_frames`.** 8 (`MIN_T`).
- **Detects.** Movement smoothness independent of amplitude/duration —
  the citable, comparable-across-different-speed-clips alternative to raw
  `mean_jerk_mps3`.
- **Does NOT detect** anything reliably across different frame rates — never
  compare `sparc` scored at one fps against another.

### `log_dimensionless_jerk`

- **Formula.** `LDLJ = -ln|DLJ|` where
  `DLJ = (duration^5 / path_len^2) * integral(||jerk||^2) dt`
  (Hogan & Sternad 2009, position form; log form from Balasubramanian et al.
  2015). Higher = smoother.
- **Units.** dimensionless. **`dt` exponent.** **`0` — provably, not merely
  measured.** `duration^5` contributes `dt`-exponent `-5`; the squared-jerk
  integral contributes `+5` (jerk is `dt^-3`, squared is `dt^-6`, one more
  `+1` from the `*dt` quadrature weight); `path_len`'s own `dt` **cancels
  exactly** against the `/dt` inside the speed used to compute it. Net:
  exactly `dt^0`, algebraically — verified to the same `rtol=1e-6` as every
  other declared metric by
  `tests/test_metric_registry_conformance.py`. **Direction.** higher_better.
  **`min_frames`.** 8.
- **Detects.** The same smoothness concept as `sparc`, but via a
  jerk-magnitude cost rather than a spectral cutoff — and is the one metric
  in the package proven, not just declared, `dt`-invariant.
- **Does NOT detect.** Whether the underlying motion is kinematically
  feasible for the robot (a smoothly-executed impossible motion still scores
  well) — same limitation as raw jerk, in dimensionless form.

## feasibility — `src/kinescore/metrics/feasibility.py`

Mechanical feasibility: self-collision, CoM balance, no-teleport. Three of
the four are gated on a robot capability (`Capability.COLLIDERS` /
`Capability.SUPPORT_POLYGON`) and are `NaN` with reason
`missing_input:{colliders,support_polygon}` — **never `0.0`** — for a robot
lacking it (e.g. the table-mounted Franka has neither): reporting `0.0`
"perfect balance"/"no penetration" for a robot that was never checked would
be a *stronger, false* claim than simply not measuring it.

### `penetration_mm`

- **Formula.** `mean(max_per_frame_sphere_sphere_penetration) * 1000` — arm
  capsule spheres (built from the FK keypoint chain) against body-core
  spheres, plus the two arms' capsules against each other.
- **Units.** mm. **`dt` exponent.** `0` (no time derivative). **Direction.**
  lower_better. **Requires.** `q`, `colliders` capability.
- **Detects.** How deeply the arm(s) pass through the torso/base/head or each
  other, in a given frame.
- **Does NOT detect.** Finger/hand self-collision (meshes are skipped by
  design — arm↔torso / arm↔arm is the target hallucination mode, not finger
  self-collision); `NaN`, never `0.0`, for a robot without `COLLIDERS`.

### `self_collision_frac`

- **Formula.** `mean((penetration > pen_eps_m).float())`,
  `pen_eps_m=1e-3` m default.
- **Units.** fraction. **`dt` exponent.** `0` — a **spatial** threshold
  (unlike the velocity/acceleration violation fractions elsewhere in the
  package, which threshold a `dt`-dependent quantity; this one doesn't
  involve `dt` at all, so it's a legitimate, checkable `dt_exponent=0`, not a
  `None`). **Direction.** lower_better. **Requires.** `q`, `colliders`.
- **Detects.** The fraction of frames with a self-collision above the
  tolerance.
- **Does NOT detect.** Severity of penetration when it occurs (that's
  `penetration_mm`); `NaN`, never `0.0`, without `COLLIDERS`.

### `com_margin_m`

- **Formula.** Signed centre-of-mass margin to the support-polygon rectangle
  (foot positions ± a half-size margin); positive = inside (stable), negative
  = tipping.
- **Units.** m. **`dt` exponent.** `0`. **Direction.** **higher_better** (more
  margin = further from the tipping boundary). **Requires.** `q`,
  `support_polygon`.
- **Detects.** Balance stability of a bipedal/humanoid robot (GR-1).
- **Does NOT detect.** Anything for a robot without `SUPPORT_POLYGON` (a
  table-mounted Franka structurally cannot fall over — `NaN`, never a
  fabricated "perfectly balanced" `0.0`/positive value); on GR-1 specifically,
  the support polygon itself is **constant across `q`** — the feet are not
  reachable from the 17 predicted joints (see `robots/gr1/spec.py`'s module
  docstring) — so this metric tracks only how the (moving) arms shift the
  whole-body CoM relative to a fixed footprint, not any actual foot placement.

### `no_teleport_frac`

- **Formula.** Per frame, `max` keypoint Cartesian speed `||dP/dt||`; `mean`
  of `(that max > v_kp_max).float()` over frames. `v_kp_max=2.0` m/s default.
- **Units.** fraction. **`dt` exponent.** **`None`** — thresholds a
  `dt`-dependent Cartesian speed against a fixed physical constant; same
  reasoning as `accel_violation_frac`/`vel_violation_frac`. **Direction.**
  lower_better. **Requires.** `P` only (no robot capability needed — the one
  feasibility metric available for every embodiment, Franka included).
- **Detects.** A keypoint physically teleporting between frames faster than
  any real robot joint could move it.
- **Does NOT detect** anything reliably at the wrong frame rate (same
  absolute-threshold sensitivity as the other violation fractions).

## Aggregates (not per-clip metrics, but consumed the same way)

These live in `src/kinescore/reference/` and `src/kinescore/bench/`, not
`src/kinescore/metrics/`, and are not in the 28-metric registry, but every
caveat here matters just as much when reading a headline number.

- **PIS** (`reference/normalize.py::invariance_score`) — aggregates the 10
  `invariant_keys` above into one `[0,1]` score. Comparable **only within one
  `suite_id`** (`MetricSuite.suite_id` — a hash of the exact declared term
  set; see [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md) D3(a)) and **only against a
  reference built at the same `dt`** (`RateMismatchError` — see D2). Two PIS
  numbers from different suites or different reference rates are not the same
  benchmark, full stop, regardless of how similar the numbers look.
- **KFD** (`reference/distances.py::kfd_approx`/`kfd`) — a Fréchet distance
  over the **raw `[mean, std]` feature vector** of each `quantity_keys`
  motion sample, pooled across a *set* of rollouts. **Not scale-invariant**:
  a speed feature at half the frame rate is (up to noise) exactly 2x the
  "same" motion's feature at full rate (`dt_exponent=1`), so it is
  **meaningless** to compare a KFD computed at one `dt` against one at
  another — not merely noisier, a different linear rescaling of the feature
  space entirely.
- **`kfd_approx` specifically** symmetrises the covariance product
  (`0.5*(prod + prod.T)`) before an `eigh`-based matrix square root, which
  equals the true `sqrtm(cov1 @ cov2)` **only when the two covariances
  commute** (e.g. either is a multiple of the identity, or they share an
  eigenbasis) — not true in general for two arbitrary real motion
  covariances. It is a cheap (no-scipy), honestly-named
  **approximation** — use `kfd` (needs the `bench` extra, `scipy.linalg.sqrtm`)
  for the exact value when the two need to agree.
